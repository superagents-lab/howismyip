## Design Context

### Users

People who want to understand an IP quickly from one place. They may be technical, but the primary interface should not require knowledge of provider credentials, deployment configuration, or internal implementation details.

### Brand Personality

Direct, investigative, and precise. The product should feel like a focused terminal tool with the clarity of a public utility, not a configuration dashboard.

### Aesthetic Direction

Keep the compact, dark terminal presentation and restrained phosphor accent. Prefer concise, action-led language and visible source transparency. Avoid decorative complexity and infrastructure terminology on the public surface.

### Design Principles

- Lead with the user's action: enter an IP and understand it.
- Express multi-source coverage as a complete view, not as technical setup copy.
- Keep public navigation obvious, including a visible link to the source repository.
- Remove labels that describe deployment mechanics rather than user value.
- Preserve the compact terminal rhythm across desktop and mobile.
